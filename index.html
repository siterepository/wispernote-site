Three moving parts.

  1. 01

    You hold the key.

    Audio is captured only while the key is down. Release it and the microphone closes. Nothing is recorded when the key is up.

  2. 02

    We transcribe while you talk.

    Your audio streams to Deepgram as you speak, so the transcript is ready almost as soon as you stop. Your dictionary is sent with it, so your names come back spelled right.

  3. 03

    We clean it, then type it.

    Anthropic's model removes the fillers, applies your corrections, punctuates it, and matches the tone to the app you are in. Then Wisper Note types the result at your cursor.

It edits what you said. It does not write something you didn’t — and we measure that. How we measure it →

Where your voice goes.

Wisper Note is not a local product and we will not pretend it is. Your audio leaves your device, gets transcribed, gets cleaned up, and comes back as text. Here is every company that touches it and what each one gets.

  • Audio is never written to disk. It stays in memory and is cleared when you stop.
  • We do not train anything on what you say.
  • Privacy mode turns off every kind of saving. Nothing is written to history, no term is learned, no usage event is sent.

Good questions.

Does it work in every app?

On macOS and Windows it types wherever your cursor is: any text field, in any app, with no plugin. Where an app refuses synthetic keystrokes — a password field, or anything running as administrator on Windows — Wisper Note copies the text and tells you to press ⌘V. That is a visible state, not a silent failure.

How is this different from the dictation already on my Mac?

macOS Dictation transcribes what you said, on device, and punctuates it. It does not delete your “um”, it does not apply “no, Tuesday”, it does not know your colleagues’ names, and it does not write differently in Slack than in Gmail. That second half is what Wisper Note is. The full comparison →

Where does my audio go?

To Deepgram, to be turned into text, while the key is down. The text then goes to Anthropic to be cleaned up. Neither gets anything else, audio is never written to disk, and privacy mode turns off every kind of saving. Details →

Does it work offline?

No. Wisper Note needs a connection to transcribe. If you are offline the pill says so and nothing is captured. A local model is on the roadmap and is not in this version.

Will it make things up?

It is not supposed to, and we test for exactly that. Every release runs a 60-utterance set through the cleanup prompt and fails if any output contains a run of three or more words that was not in the input. The method →

What does it cost?

Free for 2,000 words a week. Pro is $12 a month, or $120 a year, for unlimited words and sync across your devices. Pricing →

Do I need an account?

Yes, a free one. Transcription runs through our servers, and the app gets a short-lived key for it after you sign in. The download itself is not gated: you get the file first, and sign in inside the app.
